Translation Chaos Engine

In collaboration with Random Meow

A program that creates hilarious translation errors through chaotic processing

Release Date: April 15, 2026

The Translation Chaos Engine is a new program written in HTML and Google Apps Script by ThatCodingFrog and Random Meow. It takes a user's input text, runs it through a translation program many times, then outputs the final funny result. For additional chaos, add settings such as "Copy Lines while Translating" and "Add Random Phrases to Translation".

Features:

  • Customizable number of translations (up to 200 iterations)
  • Option to copy lines while translating, creating a longer and more chaotic output
  • Option to add random phrases to the translation process, increasing the likelihood of hilarious errors
  • Easy history and copy functionality to share your outputs with your friends easily
  • Simple user interface for easy input and settings adjustment
  • Page translations for five different languages

WARNING: You will have to authenticate with your Google Account to use the Translation Chaos Engine. To authenticate, hit "Review Permission" when prompted, then go to "Advanced" and verify that you trust the developer (me).

Example output from the Translation Chaos Engine:

Original Text: On a quiet Tuesday afternoon, a small bakery on a cobblestone street discovered that its ancient espresso machine had developed a peculiar habit: it would only work when someone hummed old sea shanties nearby. At first, the barista thought it was a coincidence, but soon regular customers began arriving not just for coffee, but to perform increasingly elaborate musical numbers in exchange for cappuccinos. A retired librarian brought sheet music, a teenager brought a ukulele, and a passing tourist insisted on tap-dancing every order. The bakery became strangely famous, not for its pastries, but for the unexpected orchestra of everyday people trying to convince a stubborn machine to wake up.

Translated Output: At first, the staff was confused, but soon customers began to choose cappuccinos with coffee and listen to modern music. On a quiet Tuesday, a strange sound came from the old espresso machine in a small brick bakery on a quiet street. The customers were expecting coffee, not bread. As the machine turned on, a beautiful woman in a swimsuit entered the shop. While the customers listened to light music, she smiled and played the ukulele. The bread was long.
